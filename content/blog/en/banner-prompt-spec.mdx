Most LinkedIn banners are either a canvas template thousands of other people are also using, or a one-shot AI image that came out glowing blue with a robot in it.

This is a third option: a fixed house style you can regenerate as many times as you want, where the only things that change are the ones you pick.

## How to use it

1. Paste the whole spec into ChatGPT, Gemini or any ai image generator of your choice. Nothing else, no extra instructions.
2. Answer the interview, or don't. It asks one question at a time and names a recommended pick for each. You can go through them one by one, answer in your own words, say "your pick" for a single question, or say "choose the rest for me" and let it decide everything that is left. Every example on this page shows the options that made it. If you like one of them, you can just use the same combination instead of choosing everything by yourself.
3. Check the recap. It lists every locked option plus the exact prop the figure will hold before anything gets drawn. Fix it here, not after.
4. Confirm, and it generates once.
5. Crop. If the model can run code it delivers the exact platform size. If not, it tells you the pixel band to cut, e.g. `keep y=320–704, full width`.

Your copy is the only text that appears in the image, so write the headline before you start. Nine words maximum, and it must not repeat your name or job title, since both already sit under the banner on the profile.

## The options

One option locked per category. Blending them is what turns a house style back into a generated image.

| Category           | Options                                                                       |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Platform           | X header 1500×500, LinkedIn cover 1584×396, or custom                         |
| Layout             | L1 text left / art right, L2 art left / text right, L3 type only, L4 art only |
| Art family         | M marble sculpture, P painted storybook                                       |
| Art direction      | A1–A6 within marble, B1–B6 within painted                                     |
| Subject effect     | E0 clean cutout, E1 particle, E2 halftone, E3 engraving, E4 blueprint         |
| Palette            | P1 rust, P2 purple, P3 cobalt, P4 forest                                      |
| Background texture | F1 flat, F2 paper grain, F3 grainy gradient                                   |
| Typography         | T1 neo-grotesk, T2 soft geometric                                             |

> All the banners on this page are one-shot results. I used ChatGPT Pro and generated each one only one time. I did not ask for any fix after that.

That is also why some of the image sizes are not the same. The model does not always give you the exact size you asked for. If the first result is not what you want, just ask again and tell it what to change.

## Author's note

One thing you should know before you use it.

This prompt has a fixed set of variants. If many people use it without changing anything, the banners will start to look like each other. Same twelve art directions, same four palettes, same five effects.

So think of this as a starting point and as inspiration. It gives you a working system, and you can change any part of it to fit yourself.

> If you are happy with the result as it is, that is completely fine. You do not have to change anything.

But if you want to make it your own, here is what I did.

1. **Go to Pinterest first and look at art.** I searched for marble sculpture art and for painted classical art, and I saved the ones I liked. Then I searched for effects, like halftone dots or particle dissolve, to see how other designers use them.

   One important thing here. Use those images as a reference only, never as something to copy. The images on Pinterest belong to the people who made them, and many of them are not free to use. So look at them to understand the style you want, describe that style in your own words, and let the AI generate something new for you.

2. **Write down what you see.** When you find an art style you like, describe it in the same shape as the directions already in the prompt: what the figure is doing, how close the camera is, what the surface looks like, what the light does. Then add it to the list as a new option.

3. **Change the colors.** The four palettes are only four lines of hex values. Every other rule in the prompt talks about color by its role (field, ink, secondary, accent), not by its name, so you can replace the hex codes and nothing else breaks.

The one thing I would keep is the structure: one option per category, no mixing, and the interview before it generates. The options are just taste. The rules are what keep the result consistent.

## Before you run it

I have only tested this on ChatGPT. It is written to be model-agnostic and should work elsewhere, but treat that as untested rather than true.

Check the spelling on every word in the output. Text rendering is still the weak point, and the fix is to regenerate rather than to accept it.

Expect the model to want to fill empty space with labels, tiny UI, and icon rows. The spec bans all of it, and it still tries. If you see a word you did not write, regenerate.

The banner sits above your name on a profile people read in two seconds. Fewer slots, bigger field, one clear line.
